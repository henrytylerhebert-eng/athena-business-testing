Athena Research Journey and Decision Trail

How each question led to the next

1. Original thesis

Starting point

Athena begins with a student-empowerment idea: students should understand where they are, what comes next, and when they need help.

What changed

The concept initially risked being too broad. “Student advising” contains many users, workflows, systems, and institutional buyers.

Question created

What exact student problem is painful, frequent, measurable, and suitable for a small test?

2. Reconstruct the advising problem

Starting point

Research reframed the problem from lack of information to lack of usable decision clarity. Students already have portals, audits, emails, policies, and advisors—but must synthesize them.

What changed

The product hypothesis narrowed toward translation and prioritization rather than another system of record.

Question created

Which student segment experiences the strongest combination of pain, urgency, actionability, and measurable consequence?

3. Compare student segments

Starting point

The analysis compared first-year students, first-generation students, student athletes, transfer students, major changers, probation students, and near-graduation students.

What changed

Transfer students emerged as the strongest general segment because credit applicability, fragmented records, and time-sensitive registration decisions create visible consequences.

Question created

Can this segment be narrowed enough to test in one semester at a real institution?

4. Validate at UL Lafayette

Starting point

Institutional research mapped UL Lafayette leadership, advising handoffs, Banner, DegreeWorks, ULink, Student Success Manager, transfer-credit processes, and procurement constraints.

What changed

The broad transfer segment narrowed to first-semester transfers with 30–60 credits and at least one unresolved equivalency or fallthrough credit.

Question created

What is the smallest product and smallest data footprint that can test the behavior?

5. Define the smallest useful experience

Starting point

The work converged on three panels: My Position, My Next Three Moves, and My Blockers, with advisor-verified actions and no automated academic decisions.

What changed

This is a decision-clarity layer, not a replacement for advisors, Banner, DegreeWorks, or professional judgment.

Question created

Can a concierge prototype or secure extract improve accuracy, speed, confidence, and completion of one correct action?

6. Design the pilot

Starting point

The proposed progression is baseline observation, prototype usability testing, then a behavioral pilot during a registration advising window.

What changed

The core proof is behavioral: can the student identify and complete the right next action with fewer corrections and less system switching?

Question created

Which go-to-market entry point should be tested first, and what desirability evidence is required before building?

7. Reopen the customer question

Starting point

Later discussion introduced community colleges and university recruiters as potentially faster, lighter-weight entry points.

What changed

These are credible strategic options, but they represent different customers, workflows, data, and value propositions.

Question created

Which option best preserves Athena’s core thesis while creating the fastest credible learning loop?

The logic in one line

Broad student empowerment → advising decision clarity → transfer-student pain → UL Lafayette workflow → minimal student view → concierge/extract pilot → compare entry markets without losing the core thesis.

Important interpretation boundary

The source reports mix directly sourced facts, institution-reported practices, literature-based findings, and strategic inference. The evidence register should be used before treating any strategic conclusion as established fact.
